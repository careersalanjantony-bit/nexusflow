How I got here

I started where most good sysadmins start: on a support desk, closing tickets that other people had already given up on. That's a useful place to learn, because it teaches you the difference between a fix that makes the alert go away and a fix that makes the problem go away.

Since then I've specialised in managed hosting infrastructure — the layer where thousands of customer sites share a fleet of Linux boxes, and where one bad Apache config, one unpatched CMS or one saturated disk becomes a lot of angry people very quickly. It's unglamorous, high-stakes work and I like it.

Today my work splits three ways. Escalations: L2/L3 tickets that need real root-cause analysis rather than a restart. Security: hardening servers before something happens, and running a calm, documented incident response when it does. Migrations: moving live workloads between servers, panels and providers without the customer ever noticing.

How I work

  • Read before you touch. Logs, monitoring history and change records come before any command that modifies state.
  • Backup before you break. No destructive operation goes ahead without a verified, restorable copy — and I've tested the restore, not just the backup job.
  • Rollback plan first. The maintenance window doesn't open until the "how do we undo this" step is written down.
  • Least privilege by default. Root is a last resort, not a starting point. Same for open ports and firewall exceptions.
  • Write it down. Every non-trivial incident ends in an RCA the customer can actually read. Undocumented fixes come back.
  • Automate the second time. Do it manually once to understand it, then script it so it's consistent forever.
